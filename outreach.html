05 / Outreach

Outreach

Astronomy becomes more interesting when people are given a reason to ask the next question themselves.

Astronomy
Public engagement

Making science something people can enter.

Public engagement has been a consistent part of my academic life. I enjoy the point where technically serious science has to be re-expressed clearly enough that someone outside the field can see why it matters — without turning it into empty spectacle.

That has included public astronomy evenings, talks, observing sessions and projects linking local history with the development of astronomy.

Selected projects
01

Astronomy in the City

At the University of Birmingham I helped organise and deliver Astronomy in the City, a recurring programme combining short talks on current astronomy with opportunities for visitors to observe the night sky and engage directly with researchers.

Birmingham astronomy
02

Panzano & the history of astronomy

My home area has links to Gian Domenico Cassini and Geminiano Montanari. I collaborated with designer Alberto Lot and local organisations on an event connecting that seventeenth-century astronomical heritage with modern astronomy.

03

Science communication

I remain interested in how astronomy, gravitational waves and modern physics can be used to open conversations with pupils and the wider public — especially when the real scientific process is visible rather than hidden behind polished conclusions.

04

Into teaching

Moving into school physics has made outreach less separate from my day-to-day work. The same challenge remains: how do you make someone want to know more after the explanation has finished?
