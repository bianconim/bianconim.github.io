About

I am a physicist and teacher based in Birmingham, with a research background in observational astrophysics and a long-standing interest in how science is communicated.

From galaxies to the classroom.

I trained as an astrophysicist and spent more than a decade working on galaxy evolution, galaxy clusters, gravitational lensing and multi-messenger astronomy. My research took me from the dynamics of the intracluster medium to the search for strongly lensed gravitational-wave sources.

In 2025 I moved into secondary physics education. I now teach physics in Birmingham, bringing the habits of research — modelling, uncertainty, evidence, curiosity and the willingness to revise an explanation — into the classroom.

I obtained my PhD in Physics at the University of Innsbruck in 2016, working in observational galaxy evolution. I subsequently held research posts in the UK, including at Nottingham and Birmingham, and collaborated across large international astronomy programmes.

Outside the neat boxes

Science has never been the only thing.

I have always liked the parts of academic life that happen outside a conventional office: observatories, mountain workshops, long conversations between talks and public events where people encounter astronomy for the first time.

A

Alpine Cosmology

I took part in the Alpine Cosmology Workshop, where cosmology and cluster physics were discussed while moving between mountain huts in the Alps — a format designed to make the conversation continue well beyond the formal talks.

B

100 km del Passatore

I completed the 100 km del Passatore ultramarathon between Florence and Faenza. It remains one of my more unreasonable but memorable experiments in persistence.
